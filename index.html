An article preview: how to compare anything fairly.

Three Questions Before You Compare

When several choices look similar, it helps to slow down. First, ask what problem you are trying to solve. Second, list the two or three things that truly matter to you. Third, look for evidence instead of impressions: tests, reviews from people in your situation, and plain facts.

Writing your criteria down before you start keeps you from being pulled around by whichever option is described most loudly.

Did you know?

Reading aloud helps memory

Hearing and seeing words together gives your brain two routes to remember them. This is called the production effect.

Breaks improve focus

Short pauses during study let your mind consolidate what it just learned, so the next session starts stronger.

Teaching is learning

Explaining an idea to someone else quickly shows you which parts you have not fully understood yet.

Find the Right Solution

Common learning problems and the idea that helps.

"I forget what I read."

Pause after each section and summarise it in one sentence. Review those sentences the next day.

"I do not know where to begin."

Start with the question, not the topic. A clear question tells you what to look for and what to ignore.

"There is too much information."

Limit yourself to three good sources. Depth from a few trusted places beats shallow skimming of many.
